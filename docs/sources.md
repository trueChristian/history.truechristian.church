# Sources and acknowledgements

## Martyrs’ Mirror

The owner supplied the Gutenberg HTML ZIP for *The Bloody Theatre, or Martyrs Mirror of the Defenseless Christians*, by Thieleman J. van Braght, in the English translation by Joseph F. Sohm. The Gutenberg record is https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/65855.

The import preserves the complete HTML edition and all 45 image files in `sources/martyrs-mirror/`. It extracts 1,272 heading-based sections from the translator’s preface onward, including accounts, letters, beliefs, chapter context, and the source’s index/notes. The original file retains its title matter, table of contents, and Gutenberg notices.

Original HTML SHA-256: `5c06ed230bf8ce32bedfee70b983aba1ae4cdf50893129fbac6fae3299cb2e10`.

Source articles preserve historical text. Navigation titles may use title case, while the exact original heading is shown in the source details. Original page markers and links lead into the intact edition. Internal footnote links point to that complete edition so they continue working when the text is divided into pages.

Date extraction uses an explicit date in the heading, otherwise the containing century. A century range is not silently replaced by a guessed event year. The supplied Felix Mantz heading retains 1526; the separately sourced editorial overview gives 5 January 1527 and explains the difference.

Scripture, Martyrs’ Mirror, and the supplied Anabaptist history material form the archive’s foundation. The source wording is preserved and its century accounts are integrated into the vertical timeline.

## Church History photographs

The supplied ZIP contains 40 HEIC photographs (`IMG_9360` through `IMG_9399`) and associated Live Photo movie files. The stills were converted to JPEG, preserving orientation and fitting within 2,000 × 2,000 pixels for web display. The movies do not add separate timeline evidence and are not deployed.

The photographs include eight historical exhibit panels, four related exhibit photographs, timeline overviews, close-ups of branch lines, world events, and church events. Each published photograph retains its original filename stem and is listed in `content/photographs.json`. The photographs remain repository research records and are excluded from the published assets. The site presents the chart information through its own vertical navigation and historical articles.

The owner identifies the timeline material as public domain. The photographer’s identity and the designer of the timeline are not inferred. Published photographs are described as supplied by the site owner. The archive names the Amish & Mennonite Heritage Center as the photographed facility and provides the individual photograph as the reference for timeline-derived research requests.
